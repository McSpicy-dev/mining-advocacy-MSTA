What is mining, and what do we need it for?

Mining is the process of extracting useful materials from the earth. Some examples of substances that are mined include coal, gold, or iron ore. Iron ore is the material from which the metal iron is produced.

These profitable substances that are mined from the earth are called minerals. A mineral is typically an inorganic substance that has a specific chemical composition and crystal structure. The minerals are valuable in their pure form, but in the earth they are mixed with other, unwanted rocks and minerals. This mix of rock and minerals is usually carried away from the mine together, then later processed and refined to isolate the desired mineral.

Why should we care?

Water water WATER

Mining and quarrying uses a LOT of water, not only just for mining, but also for processing said minerals and byproducts. The increased demand for water may compete with other water-critical use-cases such as:

  • Water for human consumption (drinking water, tap water for washing, hygienic purposes, etc)
  • Agriculture (watering the fields that feed us)
  • Livestock (cows, pigs, chicken)
  • Nature itself (wild animals and plants)

Including competition for water, it also contributes to water pollution... the same water that we need to drink and wash with. We already worry about microplastics in our bodies, but heavy metal poisoning is even worse... which has been linked to cancer and neurological (brain and nerve) damage, among other things.

More reasons to care?

How clean is the air you breathe?

Mining, quarrying, and processing are partly responsible for localized air pollution in the areas they operate in. This severely impacts not only the communities that live nearby, but also nearby farmlands, which negatively hurts our food security.

Agencies such as the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) and its Environmental Management Bureau (EMB) regulate air quality. As per their report in 2025, the quality of the air we breathe has gone up, but the impact of pollution due to mining and extraction is still significant.

What can we do?

While mining is a necessity to modern living, which means we have to live with both the modern advancements and consequences of such, there are still things we can do.

Reduce

Buy only what you must. Make sure it fits your needs. You (likely) don't need that new phone/car yet.

Reuse/Keep using

Don't upgrade if you don't have to. Your phone/computer will let you know if you need to. Your wallet will thank you.

Repair

If something breaks, see if you can get it fixed first. It may be cheaper and more practical than going new.

Research

Avoid misinformation and hype. Almost everything is linked to mining in one way or another. Understanding that can help you make better, informed choices.

Right to Repair

You own your device. You should be able to repair it (or send it to someone who has the skills and tools to) as you see fit.

Report

Do your part. If you see illegal mining in your area, or its side effects (water shortage, pollution), don't be afraid to anonymously report it. Or go viral. The hills and trees may not have a voice, but you do.

Representatives

Reach out to your local representative. Make your voice and concerns heard. Make them work for the position they are in.

Reframe

Others will agree with you, or at least be more open to your idea, if you put mining issues in a way that affect them (electronics prices go up, drinking water go down, etc).

Respect

Not everyone will share your view (and burning passion) for mining advocacy. And that is ok. Do what you can, but don't be the next one to go viral for all the wrong reasons.
